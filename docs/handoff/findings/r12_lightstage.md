## 3. [major] The 40 wing-spar strobes float off the wings: they still use the pre-round-3 spar layout (module lights, lens: MAINSTAGE, WORLD, SCALE & LIGH)
src/lighting/rig.ts lines 317–322 define SPARS = [(-6,15)->(-14.5,26.5), (-9,14)->(-29,28), (-12,13)->(-40.5,26.5)] on a wing plane Z −20 with a 10° lean (WING_LEAN). The strobe loop at lines 597–603 places 5/7/8 strobes per spar on those lines, at wingZ(y). Round 3 re-laid the wings in src/stage/dragon/layout.ts wingLayout(): the outer and middle spars rise near-vertically from a low wrist (bases (±36.2, 6.8, −9.8), (±27.6, 5.2, −9.0)) to finials at Z ≈ −21, leaning back ~30°. The strobe rows therefore run diagonally across the membranes and into the sky above the scalloped top edge, not along any spar. The moving heads follow the registered fixtures_truss geometry; the strobes do not. All 32 `strobe` cues in the show have no target (e.g. 75.939, 338.881, 483.355, 513.743 …), so every hit fires these misplaced sources.
Evidence: Daylight wing close-up, pose (-22, 24, 40, yaw 0, pitch −0.05, FOV 14), t=700: .shots/qa12-day-wingtopL.png shows about 10 red strobe bodies hanging in the sky and in front of the membrane (e.g. px (390,103), (505,186), (1088,181)). The same pose with off=lights (.shots/qa12-day-wingtopL-nolights.png) has none. The same red bodies appear around the head in .shots/qa12-day-headcl.png. At night, at the strobe hit t=338.9, pose (-22, 1.7, 70), .shots/qa12-strobe339.png shows the white strobe discs forming straight diagonal rows from the head out to the tips across the left wing. Video strobe hits (e.g. frame 1356 at 339 s) light the spars and the membrane edges.
Suggested fix: Build the T_SPAR strobe positions from wingLayout(side): interpolate bases[i] → tips[i] (or the registered fixtures_truss T_SPAR positions, like the heads), offset 0.25 m to the audience side of the spar, facing +Z. Delete the SPARS / WING_LEAN constants, or derive them from dragon/layout.ts so the two cannot drift again.

## 4. [major] The wing membranes are under-lit and the castle over-lit: the brightness hierarchy is inverted against the video (module stage, lens: MAINSTAGE, WORLD, SCALE & LIGH)
In the video the wings are the dominant light surfaces: the printed membranes are fully lit in saturated red, pink or blue with white spar plates, the dragon head is equally bright, and the castle core and side sections are a dim band. We light mainly the LED outlines, rosettes and festoon bulbs while the membrane surfaces stay dark, and the white-painted castle stone and banners read brighter than filmed. Result: our stage reads as 'outlined wireframe wings over a lit castle' instead of 'glowing wings over a dark castle'. Linear luminance, video vs ours, Show camera (region boxes on the 640-px sheet halves): 176 s wings 0.076 vs 0.008 (10x), 288 s wings 0.077 vs 0.034 and castle 0.016 vs 0.045 (2.7x too bright), 592 s wings 0.19/0.15 vs 0.12/0.12 and castle 0.092 vs 0.135, 1503 s wings 0.108 vs 0.055, 1507 s wings 0.072–0.091 vs 0.014–0.016 (5–6x) and castle 0.017 vs 0.033. Caveat: at 288 and 592 s the castle box also contains haze.
Evidence: Compare sheets /Users/agvastgoedmedia/Projects/endshow-data/work/compare/qa12_wide1.jpg (176, 288 s; frames 704, 1152), qa12_wide2.jpg (592 s, frame 2368), qa12_176seq.jpg (176.5–180 s, frames 706–720), qa12_1500s.jpg (1503, 1507 s; frames 6012, 6028). A zoom of the video wings at 176.5 and 592 s shows full membrane fill; our 592 render (.shots/qa12-tele592.png, .shots/qa12-terr592.png) shows dark membranes behind thick festoon strings.
Suggested fix: Give the wing membrane shader a show-driven emissive fill: StageLook wing colour × a pixel-mapped pattern that follows the painted inferno texture's flame lanes, at 0.4–0.7 of the LED-line level, cued by the same stage looks. Add a front wash from the deck and roof rows onto the membranes. Lower the castle stone's night albedo or wash share (nightK) by about 0.5 so the core and side sections sit below the wings. Verify with the region luminances above and the similarity 'light' part on 176/288/592/1507.
