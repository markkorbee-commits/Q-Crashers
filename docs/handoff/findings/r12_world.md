The festival was cancelled and the other areas were dark: in the video the right-hand horizon shows only low coloured light clusters and no beams. Seen at v44.75, 55.5, 62, 93.5, 142.5, 243.5, 244.5, 550, 562, 600, 615, 870, 1290, 1440, 1490, 1555 and 1570.

## 2. [major] Wet puddles on the paving contradict the dry night and read as noisy mirror blobs in the default view (module world, lens: MAINSTAGE, WORLD, SCALE & LIGH)
Terrain.ts sets uWet = 1 and paints damp gutters and 'low spots still wet from the shower before the storm (INFERENCE)', with roughness 0.18 and sky and moon reflection. Bible §8.4 says 'Dry during the show' and 'no dew'. The storm rain came 23:00–24:00, after the show, following a 31 °C afternoon and a 36.8 °C day before. The damp patches render as sharp-edged, speckled mirror blobs. One sits right on the axis at z≈80 and draws the eye at the bottom centre of the default 'middle' start spot and of every axis view.
Evidence: Default spot: .shots/qa12-spot-middle.png (bottom centre, speckled bright patch). .shots/qa12-walk-z75.png (red mirror blob at px 640,640), .shots/qa12-q-ultra.png (violet blob on the axis at t=592), .shots/qa12-puddle-x10.png (a second patch at px 950,530). No wet reflections are visible on the field in any wide video frame (e.g. frames 2368, 4608).
Suggested fix: Set uWet to 0 by default, or keep only the gully grates slightly darker with roughness ≥ 0.6. If some sheen is wanted, use a very low-contrast, broad, rough 'warm concrete' specular with no mirror reflection and no hard mask edges.

## 3. [major] MOBILE stage/dragon merged materials bake a neutral grey albedo: texMean() calls a three.js function that does not exist (module stage, lens: Technical quality, performance)
src/stage/materials/texMean.ts:25 uses THREE.SRGBToLinear, which three r186 defines internally (three.core.js:6882) but does not export. Every call with an sRGB texture throws inside the try block, and texMean silently returns the fallback [0.5,0.5,0.5]. The production build reports it: '[IMPORT_IS_UNDEFINED] Import SRGBToLinear will always be undefined' (npx vite build). The call sites are the MOBILE-only merges: StageMaterials.ts:207-209 (grain map, orm, grille for stage-setmix) and DragonCrown.ts:634-635 (toLiteMix mean albedo and roughness for crown-jaw-mix and crown-static-mix). High and medium don't use the merges (verified: no '*mix' materials on high or medium). Measured in page, texMean against the correct sRGB-decoded mean: stage-decor 0.5 vs (0.398,0.197,0.123), crown-static-shell 0.5 vs (0.245,0.128,0.108), crown-membranes 0.5 vs (0.417,0.086,0.011), stage-stone 0.5 vs (0.541,0.520,0.474). Every other sRGB map in the scene returns 0.5 too. Visible result at night (t=700, free cam 0,12,62): on mobile the black speaker stacks and side hangs turn into lighter red-grey slabs and the dragon crown loses contrast and reads flat. In daylight (dev) the speakers are light grey instead of black. Phone users (the whole mobile preset) see a washed-out stage and dragon.
Evidence: npx vite build --outDir .../work/qa-dist: IMPORT_IS_UNDEFINED warning at texMean.ts:25. In-page check (texmean.mjs, quality=mobile): texMean() = [0.5,0.5,0.5] for all 22 sRGB maps listed above; colorSpace 'srgb'. Renders: .shots/qa12-night-high.png vs .shots/qa12-night-mobile.png (t=700 night, same pose; video ref f4/02800.jpg); .shots/qa12-dragon-day2-high.png vs .shots/qa12-dragon-day2-mobile.png (daylight, speakers black vs light grey). Mix materials present on mobile only: stage-setmix, crown-jaw-mix, crown-static-mix.
Suggested fix: In texMean use a real decode: const c = new THREE.Color().setRGB(d[i]/255, d[i+1]/255, d[i+2]/255, THREE.SRGBColorSpace) (this decodes into the linear working space), or inline v<=0.04045 ? v/12.92 : ((v+0.055)/1.055)**2.4. Keep the try/catch only around drawImage/getImageData, so a code error can no longer masquerade as 'not drawable'. After the fix, re-check the mobile mix tints in StageMaterials (rel() divides by the grain mean, which was 0.5 until now). Add a build step that fails on IMPORT_IS_UNDEFINED (e.g. vite build --logLevel check, or grep the build output in CI).

## 4. [major] Auto-picked ULTRA at Retina DPR 2 (3024x1890) exceeds the frame budget at heavy moments; the finale stretch 1510-1516 visibly drops frames even on an M4 Max (module perf, lens: Technical quality, performance)
pickQuality (src/core/Quality.ts) selects ultra for /apple m[1-9] (pro|max|ultra)/. On a DPR 2 display, ultra's maxPixelRatio 2 renders the scene at 3024x1890 (5.7 MP) into a half-float target with MSAA2 (PostFX drops to 2 at this size), with 6 bloom levels and 14 passes. A warm-GPU timer sweep (EXT_disjoint_timer_query, continuous rAF, 24 frames per point, every 6 s, spot=middle) on the M4 Max gives a median of 11.0 ms. But 33 of 264 moments exceed 16.7 ms, 3 exceed 25 ms and 1 exceeds 33 ms; worst are 1514 (37.9 ms), 878 (30.8), 1040 (25.3), 1532 (23.5), 1526 (22.4) and 500 (22.1). High at the same DPR (2268x1417, MSAA4) has a median of 5.95 ms, with only 3 of 264 over 16.7 ms (1514: 26.1, 878: 21.2, 1040: 17.5). During playback with the governor on (default visitor, no ?quality, 1512x945 at DPR 2, reproduced 3 times from t=1490): governor windows at 1510-1516 reach 18-22 ms, resolution drops to 0.9 (once to 0.8) for about 8 s, and 9-66 frames per 45 s exceed 33 ms (worst 66.7 ms). This is the 'In The Cold' red-haze stretch of the finale; video f4/06040-06064. The 878 peak is short, and ultra playback held 60 fps across 860-900. Attribution at 1512 (warm timer, one component hidden at a time): the LightingSystem group costs about 9-11 ms, though its children individually sum to only about 2 ms (an interaction, not one layer). Disabling PostFX saves 24.5 ms, but that removes the half-float MSAA scene target, not just the post passes. The pyro-glare parameters themselves (halo count, amount) cost only 0-2.5 ms. So the cost is additive overdraw (beams, haze, fog puffs, pyro) into a 5.7 MP half-float MSAA target. Inference, not measured: M1/M2/M3 Pro chips match the same regex with roughly a half to a third of this GPU, so they would sit at the lowest governor step through the finale. Context, all good: CPU is not the bottleneck (step 1.4-2.4 ms; every system ≤ 0.19 ms, far under the 1.5 ms budget). Clock, memory, load and console are all clean (see other findings).
Evidence: Warm-GPU sweeps: gsweep ultra DPR2 median 11.0 ms, >16.7 ms at 33/264, worst [1514 37.9, 878 30.8, 1040 25.3, 1532 23.5]; high DPR2 median 5.95, worst [1514 26.1, 878 21.2]. Play runs (gov.mjs, no quality param, DPR 2): run1 scale 0.9->0.8 at 1512-1516, over33=66/2155 frames, max 66.7 ms; runs 2/3 frameMs 18.1-22.1 ms at 1510-1514, scale 0.9, over33=9 and 29. Unthrottled rAF at ultra DPR2, t=1510: median 43.5 ms, p95 84 ms; high DPR2: 26.9 ms. Attribution at 1512 ultra DPR2: BASE 39.2-41.5 ms, PostFX off -24.5, LightingSystem hidden -8.7/-10.7/-11.0, glare halos/amount -0.4/+1.7/-2.5. Screenshot .shots/qa12-perf-ultra.png (t=1515, full-screen red haze). Video frames f4/06040.jpg-f4/06064.jpg.
Suggested fix: (1) Pixel budget for ultra: cap maxPixelRatio at 1.5, or clamp the effective DPR so the scene target stays ≤ ~3.5 MP (dpr = min(maxPixelRatio, sqrt(3.5e6/(cssW*cssH)))), which keeps ultra's extra content without 5.7 MP fill. (2) pickQuality: ultra only for Max/Ultra chips (and RTX-class); Pro chips get high. (3) Render the additive volumetrics (LightingSystem beams/WashGlow/FloodGlow, fog puffs, haze sprites) into a half-resolution non-MSAA offscreen buffer and composite it in PostFX (standard off-screen particles); this cuts the heavy term roughly 4x at any resolution. (4) Optional: let the governor start at step 0.8 on >4 MP canvases, so the first heavy moment doesn't cost a 2-window stall.

## 5. [major] The finale red smoke tints the whole landscape and horizon; in the video everything outside the grounds stays black (module fog, lens: MAINSTAGE, WORLD, SCALE & LIGH)
In the `atmos.glow smoke` finale (1510–1537 s) the whole sky dome and the far terrain (fields, forest belts, the land beyond the lake) take the red glow colour. Bible §7.8, round 9, limits the lit smoke to an ellipsoid over the grounds, but lets 'the whole dome glow inside an atmos.glow smoke scene'. The drone shots of the video show a red field inside black forest and black surroundings. Periphery luminance, video vs ours: 1518 s mid-left 0.0008 vs 0.0137, bottom-right 0.0018 vs 0.0164; 1522 s mid-left 0.0010 vs 0.0692 (70x). Meanwhile the video's firework and smoke band at the top is 2.3x brighter than ours (0.092 vs 0.038). The light should live over the U, not across the whole landscape.
Evidence: qa12_1500s.jpg rows 1518 and 1522 s (frames 6072 and 6088). Free drone (0, 95, 290, pitch −0.3) at t=1511: .shots/qa12-drone1511.png shows the whole landscape to the horizon and the sky red; the same pose at 592 s (.shots/qa12-drone592.png) has a violet dome over the site.
Suggested fix: In a smoke scene keep the glow bounded by the grounds ellipsoid (the round-9 volume) for sky rays. Apply the fog glow colour to terrain and trees only inside that volume: multiply by exp(−2(q²−1)) of the fragment's position, and outside it fade to the night fog colour. Use the 'whole dome' fallback only when the camera is inside the cloud and below its top, and even then not for ground fragments beyond ~200 m. Re-measure 1515–1533 with similarity; the periphery target is below 0.003.
